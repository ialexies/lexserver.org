## What makes a project cost more

A starting price covers a simple version. These are the things that move a quote up, roughly in order of how much they add.

### User accounts

The moment people need to sign up, log in and see their own data, you need password resets, permissions, account settings and more testing. This is often the biggest jump between a website and a web app.

### Payments

Taking payments means a payment gateway, order records, receipts, refunds and handling failed payments. GCash and Maya are expected by most Filipino customers, so plan for them from the start.

### Connections to other systems

Every link to another system, such as your accounting software, a courier, an SMS service or an existing database, needs to be built and then kept working when the other side changes.

### Admin tools

Your team will need screens to manage orders, approve listings or answer inquiries. These are easy to forget when budgeting and often take as long as the customer-facing side.

### More pages, more screens, more languages

Each extra page or screen is design, build and testing time. A site in both English and Filipino is close to two sites of content.

### Content you don't have yet

If we write the copy or take product photos, that's extra. If you send them late, the launch moves.

## The costs after launch

This is the part most budgets miss.

- **Domain name.** Paid every year to your domain registrar.
- **Hosting.** Paid monthly or yearly. Cheap shared hosting is fine for a small site; a web app needs more.
- **Maintenance.** WordPress, plugins and app libraries all release updates and security fixes. Someone has to apply and test them.
- **App store accounts.** Apple charges US$99 a year for a developer account. Google Play charges a one-time US$25.
- **App updates.** Apple and Google change their requirements every year. An app that isn't updated eventually gets flagged or removed from the store.
- **Third-party services.** Email sending, SMS, maps and payment gateways usually charge per use or per month.

A useful rule: if you can only just afford the build, wait until you can also afford a year of running it.
